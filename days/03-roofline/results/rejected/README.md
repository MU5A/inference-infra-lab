# Rejected results: do not cite

These files came from measurement attempts that did not hold. They are kept as evidence of what
interference looks like, and so that nothing is quietly dropped. **None of their numbers may be
used in a claim.** Day 3's citable results are the files one folder up.

| File | What was wrong |
|---|---|
| `gpu_skinny_attempt1_unstable.json` | First attempt at the decode-shaped sweep (batch 1 to 1024 times an 8192 x 8192 half-precision weight matrix). Three process runs, started after a clean pre-flight and a decode probe within 20% of the Day 1 baseline (158.2 tokens/s before, 150.4 after, no drift flagged). Batches 1 to 32 were steady (variation 0.6% to 3.1%), but batches 64, 128, 256 and 512 failed the 10% limit (19.7%, 25.3%, 21.0% and 10.1%) and batch 1024 was at 7.6%. The first two runs gave about 2.8 to 3.0 TFLOPS for batches 64 to 512 and the third gave 1.6 to 2.3, so the last run was slower, not scattered. The neighbour sampler recorded `ANECompilerService` (an Apple system process, not started by this repository) at up to 89.8% of a core, a one-minute load of 3.64, and flagged the phase contended. It was still running at about 90% when checked right after the phase ended, which is consistent with interference but does not prove it was the cause: no temperature or clock was read, and the machine is a fanless MacBook Air. |
| `gpu_skinny_attempt2_unstable.json` | Second attempt at the same sweep, with almost no rest after the first (the two result files were written at 22:10:38 and 22:12:25 local time, 1 minute 47 seconds apart, and the quiet-wait passed at once). Started after a clean pre-flight and a decode probe within the gate (158.0 tokens/s before, 155.5 after, no drift flagged). The neighbour sampler did not flag contention this time (no `ANECompilerService` in the busiest list; one-minute load peaked at 5.07; load counts every process, including this benchmark's own). Batches 1 to 32 were steady (variation 0.2% to 0.6%), but batches 64, 128 and 256 failed the 10% limit (10.6%, 11.1% and 10.4%). In all three the runs fell one after another (batch 64: 2690, 2430, 2070 GFLOPS; batch 128: 2307, 1992, 1760; batch 256: 2113, 2115, 1680), and the first run of this attempt was already below the first run of attempt 1 (2690 against 3024 at batch 64). That pattern looks like a machine slowing under sustained work. The decode probe showed no drift, and it measures a memory-bound workload, which may be why it missed this. The cause is not proven: no temperature or clock was read (`pmset -g therm` reports nothing on this chip). |
